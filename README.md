*IMPORTANT*

*LOT is an independent Home Assistant integration for organizing LEGO® sets. LOT is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by the LEGO Group. LEGO® is a trademark of the LEGO Group.*

A custom integration (HACS compatible) that tracks LEGO sets and their prices at **LEGO.com, Amazon.nl, Amazon.de, Amazon.com.be, bol.com, Kruidvat.be, Dreamland.be and your own shops**, plus the prices that **price-comparison sites** (Kieskeurig, Shoparize, Channable, Producthero) know. It has a daily deals dashboard, price charts, the market value of every set, a collection value chart, a news and price ticker, and a full logbook.

> Status: 0.9.20. 200+ unit and integration tests against a real Home Assistant core (2026.2.3). The panel is tested in Chromium (desktop and mobile, several languages) with test data generated by the integration itself. **Not done yet:** checking the shop parsers against the live sites.

## Installation and updates
1. HACS → Custom repositories → this repository, category *Integration* (or copy `custom_components/lego_tracker` into your `config/`).
2. Restart Home Assistant → Settings → Devices & services → add *LEGO Price Tracker*.
3. Choose the language, shops and price check mode; everything else can be changed later in the panel.

**After every update via HACS, restart Home Assistant.** HACS replaces the files right away, but Home Assistant keeps running the previous integration code until a restart, so the new panel would talk to the old integration and new buttons and settings would not work yet. The panel notices this and shows the banner *Restart Home Assistant to finish the update*. The browser always loads the new panel after an update (the panel address carries a fingerprint of the file).

## Languages
The panel, notifications, the userscript and the integration's messages are available in **English** (default), **Nederlands, Français, Deutsch, Español, 中文 (简体), 한국어 and Bahasa Indonesia**. Choose the language under *Manage → Settings → Language* (or in the integration options). *Automatic* follows the language of Home Assistant.

Everything is stored in English and translated when shown, so switching languages also translates older logbook lines. Translations live in `custom_components/lego_tracker/panel/i18n/<lang>.json` (`{"English text": "translation"}`); adding a language means adding one file. `python scripts/i18n_extract.py` lists missing strings, and `tests/test_i18n.py` checks that every language is complete and keeps the `{placeholders}`.

## The panel
The sidebar panel **LEGO** has four sections and a ticker at the bottom.

**🏷️ Deals & watchlist** (sets you keep an eye on)
- *Today*: deal of the day, all deals, sets retiring soon, big price drops, and recent deals, each with a thumbnail, the price, the discount, the deal score, a link to the shop and the ＋ / W buttons.
- *Watchlist*: the sets you keep an eye on (at most 100), sortable by priority (★–★★★), deal score, discount, lowest price, price per piece, biggest drop, newest or longest on the watchlist, and latest deal notification; total now vs. RRP.
- *New sets*: every new LEGO set, newest first, with picture, theme, year and pieces (see *LEGO set database and new sets*).
- *All LEGO sets*: the whole LEGO set database, with the lowest price, discount and status of every set: a deal, in the shops, no shop found, retired, or followed by you (see *Deals on every LEGO set*).
- *All prices*: everything that is tracked, with theme and subtheme filters; sortable by deal score, discount, lowest price, price per piece, biggest drop, latest deal notification, name or set number.
- *Settings*: what counts as a deal, and which sets are left out (see *Deal settings*).
- Every view has a **⏳ Retiring within 100 days** filter. The first tile of every grid is a big **＋** to add a set. Every card picture has two buttons: **＋** adds the set to your collection (the add form opens with the number and picture filled in), **W** puts it on the watchlist (also a set you own, e.g. for a second copy) and **−W** takes it off again (also a set you don't own).

**📦 My collection** (what you own)
- *Overview*: value, purchase cost, growth, pieces, paid per piece; growth chart with periods (30 days … this year … all), your own from/to dates, or drag across the chart to zoom in; for the chosen period: value change, growth without purchases, highest and lowest value, sets bought and money spent.
- Statistics: unique sets and copies, value per piece, average value and price paid, value vs. purchase price and vs. RRP, retired and retiring sets, share sealed, average age, most valuable / largest / oldest set, a table per theme (sets, value, share, growth), bought and spent per year, most valuable sets, biggest gain, best return, sets per location, value per theme, sets per release year, condition.
- *Sets*: tiles or table, filter by theme and condition (Sealed / Opened / Built / Incomplete), location, CSV export; the first tile adds a set to your collection.

**📜 Logbook** (everything the integration does), see *Logbook*.

**⚙️ Manage**: add sets, import, link check, notifications, shops & jobs, settings, userscript, backup. Under *Shops & jobs* every shop card opens the shop's details, refreshed every 5 seconds: a diagnosis (e.g. a search page that loads its results with JavaScript, bot protection, a pause), when the shop may be asked again, the shop's sitemap, and its last requests: what was asked, the HTTP status, the size, what came back or why it failed. A shop you add yourself is searched and fetched right away.

**Click any set** (cards, tables, logbook, errors, link check, ticker) to open it: price chart per shop, statistics, the **market value** (new and used) with the expected retirement date, the prices the comparison sites list for every shop, and all editable data, including every shop link and price (see *Manual values always win*). **⚠ Problem with this set** collects everything known about the set (links, prices, errors, recent log) with what you tick (link, shop, price, set details, …) and your remark, and writes it to the logbook or downloads it as CSV; *Logbook → Export → Problem reports* exports all of them.

### Ticker
The bar at the bottom of the panel shows the latest prices of your watchlist (marked **W**; big drops ⬇⬇ in bold, small drops ↓, rises ↑; without recent changes the current prices), deal notifications (marked **D**), and news about LOT. Flames show how good a price is: 🔥 good price (deal score ≥ 45), 🔥🔥 super price (≥ 70), 🔥🔥🔥 amazing price (≥ 85). Click a price or a deal to open the set, or a news item to read it in a popup (● marks news you haven't read). It is on by default with 3 items per part; under *Manage → Settings → Ticker* you switch each part on or off and choose how many items it shows (0–50). The news is read every 3 hours from `https://www.emery.be/lot/nieuws.txt` (format: see `news/nieuws.txt`; the text may follow the header lines directly or after an empty line); its links only open `https://` pages or pages inside Home Assistant.

## LEGO set database and new sets
The integration keeps a database of **every LEGO set there is** (number, name, year, theme, subtheme, pieces, picture). It is downloaded within 10 minutes after Home Assistant starts and then once a day, from the free daily set list Rebrickable publishes (about 1 MB, no key needed), and stored in its own file. Books, gear and other products without bricks are left out.
- **Adding a set**: *Manage → Add* has a search field for the database (name or number); a result fills in the number, name, theme, subtheme and pieces. Every added set gets its name, theme, year, pieces and picture from the database right away, before the slower online lookups (LEGO.com still provides the RRP and the official picture). Right after adding, the tool looks for the set in the shops and fetches its first prices and its market value, one set after the other, so you don't have to wait for the next round (the set's window updates by itself once they are in; in the logbook this check is marked *Just added*).
- **New sets**: a set that was not in yesterday's list is new. *Deals → New sets* lists them, newest first, with a search field and a theme filter; the tab shows how many came in the last 7 days. **W** puts a set on your watchlist (it is added and its prices are followed), **＋** opens the add form for your collection. Themes and piece limits switched off under *Deals → Settings* are left out. On the very first download the sets of this year count as new.
- **Notification**: the trigger *New LEGO set announced* (Manage → Notifications) sends one message per download with the new sets; a rule for certain themes only hears about those themes, and themes switched off under *Deals → Settings* never notify.

## Deals on every LEGO set
Also sets you don't follow are looked up for deals. *Deals → All LEGO sets* shows the whole database: search by name or number, filter on theme and status, and sort by biggest discount, newest, lowest price or name. **W** puts a set on your watchlist and **＋** opens the add form for your collection; from then on all its shops are followed.
- **Which sets**: sets released this year and in the three years before that are not retired and that you don't follow yet. Themes, prices and piece limits from *Deals → Settings* apply.
- **How**: one set at a time, spread over the day, on one of the price-comparison sites (one page lists the prices of many shops). Only shops LOT knows count, and a price below a quarter of the RRP is ignored (a part or a sticker, not the set). How many sets are looked up per day is set under *Deals → Settings* (off, 100, 300 (default), 600 or 1000). A set that no shop sells is looked up only every 14 days after three tries. It needs the price-comparison sites to be on.
- **Retired sets**: whether a set is retired is checked on its market value page when it is first looked up and then every 30 days. Retired sets are no longer looked up for prices and show as *Retired* with the year.
- **Deals**: a set is a deal when its lowest price is at least the discount threshold below the RRP (and within the price limits of *Deal settings*). The logbook notes every new deal, and the notification trigger *Deal on a set you don't follow* tells you about it, the first time and again when the price drops another 2 %. A rule for certain themes only hears about those themes.
- The results are kept in their own storage file, so the main data stays small.

## Deal settings
*Deals → Settings* decides what counts as a deal and which sets take part:
- **What counts as a deal**: the discount threshold, the minimum deal score (default 70), the minimum days of history for "lowest ever", and whether "lowest price ever" and "target price reached" count as a deal.
- **Which sets**: a lowest price between € x and € y, a minimum discount on the RRP, a minimum and maximum number of pieces, leave out sets you own, leave out retired sets. An empty field means no limit; amounts may be typed with a decimal comma.
- **Deals on every LEGO set**: how many sets of the database are looked up per day (see *Deals on every LEGO set*).
- **Themes**: the full list of LEGO themes, your own themes first (with the number of sets), with a search field and *All on* / *All off*. Untick a theme to leave it out. Spellings from different sources match ("Star Wars" = "star-wars").

**Left out also means no notifications.** Left-out sets don't appear under *Today* and *All prices*, don't count as a deal, and give no deal or price notifications, also not in the daily digest or the ticker. Notification rules for sets you picked one by one keep working, and the watchlist still shows them. The tab shows how many sets are left out right now.

## Manual values always win
In the set dialog, the *Shops: links & prices* table shows every shop with its link and price:
- **Change a link** by pasting a product URL or ASIN, or **add a link** for a shop that has none. A link to the same page keeps its price history; another product starts fresh.
- **Enter a price by hand.** A manual price always wins: automatic checks still run and are logged ("manual price wins; the shop said €…"), but never overwrite it.
- Manual values are marked **✎ manual**; automatic ones **auto**.
- **Empty a field to hand it back to automation**: an empty link lets *Find links* search again (the link is not blocked); an empty price switches back to the automatic price.
- Amounts accept a decimal comma or point (164,99 and 164.99). An RRP above €2000 asks for confirmation first, in case the comma got lost.

The same applies to set details (name, theme, RRP, pieces, year, image): what you change is kept for good (✎ manual), and an emptied field is filled in again automatically from LEGO.com / Brickset / Rebrickable. Saving the form only sends the fields you actually changed, so automatic values stay automatic.

The same actions are available as service `lego_tracker.fix_offer` and websocket command `lego_tracker/offer/update`.

## Fix every item by hand, shop by shop
Every item in **Logbook → Open errors**, **Manage → Link check** and the logbook itself opens into an editor (click the row), and the set dialog has the same controls:
- **Name**: change it (✎ manual) or empty it to fill it in automatically again.
- **🖼 Set data from LEGO.com**: gets the image, RRP and name from LEGO.com first, then theme, year and pieces from Brickset / Rebrickable.
- **Per shop**: a **↻ Fetch** button fetches that one shop right now, even when the shop is paused after a block. Shops without a link have **🔎 Find**, which searches that shop for the set and fetches the price straight away. **↻ Fetch all shops** does every shop of the set one after another. Each result is shown immediately and logged as a shop check.
- Per shop you can also paste the right link, enter a manual price, approve the link (✓), approve a suspicious price (**✓ Approve €x**), ignore its error (🙈) or remove it (🗑).

So when the automatic checks keep failing, you can still get prices in one set and one shop at a time. Set names are clickable everywhere, including the logbook.

**Images come from LEGO.com whenever possible**: a LEGO.com image replaces images from shops or Brickset (but never one you set yourself), and sets without a LEGO.com image are retried weekly.

## Spread checks: every set once per cycle
Under *Manage → Settings → Automatic price checks*:
- **Spread over the day** (default): every set is checked once per cycle at every shop. Choose the cycle: 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 or 24 hours. The integration checks one set every *cycle ÷ number of sets*, so 240 sets in 24 h means one set every 6 minutes, 10 sets per hour. The set that was checked longest ago goes first. This is much gentler on the shops than a big round, so there are fewer blocks.
- **Watchlist cycle**: sets on your watchlist can be checked more often: every 30 minutes, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 or 24 hours (never less often than the other sets). The settings show how many checks per hour each cycle gives, and the total.
- **Off**: only when you fetch a set yourself (↻ in the set window).

Checking every set at once (at fixed times, or with a "refresh all prices" button) is not available: a full round puts too much load on the shops and comparison sites, which then block you sooner. To fetch one set now, use ↻ in its window.

The settings and the logbook show the pace, the next check (and which set), and how many sets were checked in the last 24 hours. Paused shops are skipped, and a running manual job takes priority.

The **market value** of every set is refreshed once a day per set, spread over the whole day; sets you own store it as their current value (see *collection value* under *Settings in the panel*).

## Logbook
The **📜 Logbook** section (top level, next to your collection) keeps the last 6000 events:
- **Everything**: price changes (old → new), shop checks, links found / approved / rejected / removed / set by hand, manual and approved prices, prices from the userscript (Tampermonkey), imports, jobs with a summary per shop ("bol.com: 230 succeeded, 6 failed"), blocks, pauses and resumes, set data, notifications and your own actions.
- **Shop checks**: one line per set check with a **green / red result per shop** (green = price fetched, red = failed with the reason, grey = skipped because the shop is paused).
- **Open errors**: every current problem (suspicious link, suspicious price, blocked, page gone, price not found, no links at all), with a fix box.

**How each price was read** is shown right on the line, without opening it, followed by the shop name: **⌂** directly from the shop's own site, **ⓤ** by your own browser (userscript), **ⓢ** Shoparize, **ⓚ** Kieskeurig, **ⓒ** Channable, **ⓟ** Producthero. A legend sits above the list. The CSV export has the same information in the column *method*, and prices from the userscript are part of the shop checks export.

**Suspicious prices.** A price far from what the other shops ask, or far below or above the RRP (< 20 % or > 4×), is held back instead of counted (e.g. another product or an accessory on the page). When it is right after all, press **✓ Approve €x**: in the set window (Shops), under *Shops & jobs → Offers without a price*, under *Open errors*, or on the logbook line itself. The price counts from then on, and prices close to it (±25 %) for that link are accepted too. The filter chip **⚠ Suspicious prices** shows only those lines. An RRP that the shops clearly contradict (e.g. 16499 instead of 164,99) is not trusted for this check.

**⬇ Export (CSV)** at the top of the logbook: shop checks, price history per set and/or price history in total (per day the sum of the lowest prices of the chosen sets), and problem reports, for a period. Shop checks can be filtered on set, shop and result (succeeded / failed / skipped); price history on set, watchlist or collection, condition, priority, retiring soon, location, theme, subtheme and year.

Filters: ✓ succeeded / ✕ failed / ⚠ suspicious prices, errors & warnings / events, kind, shop, source (server, schedule, panel, userscript, import), set number and free text / link. Identical consecutive messages are merged (×count). Click a line for details and the result per shop; for a problem you can enter the correct link and/or price right there (**Fix**), try again, ignore it or remove the link. The set dialog has a 📜 button that opens the logbook filtered on that set, and the shop cards link to their failed checks.

## Search URLs
Every shop has a built-in search URL, and every search URL can be changed under *Manage → Settings → Shops*. The easiest way: search the shop for e.g. "lego 10311", copy the address bar and paste it. The set number is replaced by `{query}` automatically, and a live example link lets you test it. Placeholders: `{query}` = "LEGO + set number", `{number}` = the set number, `{locale}` = the LEGO.com country (e.g. `nl-be`). ↺ restores the default. You can also add your own shops (name, domain, search URL), e.g. Smyths Toys or Intertoys; prices are read from the standard product data (JSON-LD / meta tags) most web shops have. If the automatic search finds nothing, the set window tells you why (blocked, HTTP error, results loaded by JavaScript, or really nothing matching). You can then paste the product page, or paste a search page of that shop and press 🔎 *Find* to look for the set on that page.

### Other ways to find product links
When a shop's search finds nothing, the integration tries other routes before giving up. Every link found this way gets the same title check (set number, LEGO, no accessory, no other brand):
- **The shop's sitemap.** Shops list all their product pages for search engines (robots.txt → sitemap). Once a week the integration reads that list for every active shop (except LEGO.com, Amazon and bol.com), calmly, one file at a time, keeps the LEGO product pages and links every set whose number is in a product address. This also works for shops whose search is built with JavaScript. *Shops & jobs → click a shop* shows when it was read, how many LEGO pages it has and how many links came from it, with a *Read the sitemap now* button. Links from the sitemap are marked 🗺 in the set window.
- **A redirect to the product page.** Many shops jump straight to the product page when a search has exactly one result; that page is taken as the link.
- **The EAN (barcode).** In a *Find links* job, a set with a known EAN is also searched by its EAN.
- **Price-comparison sites.** A shop without a link gets the shop's product page (or the comparison page) that a comparison site lists for the set.
- **A background tab in your browser** (userscript, optional): shops whose search results are built with JavaScript (e.g. Smyths Toys) are opened for a few seconds in a background tab, the script reads the finished page and the tab closes again (see *Continuous check*).

## Buttons and jobs
**🔎 Find links** is always at the top; *Manage → Shops & jobs* also has **ℹ️ Fill in set data**, and *Manage → Settings → Price-comparison sites* has **↻ Fetch all comparison sites for all sets**. Every button starts a **background job**:
- Only one job runs at a time. A bar shows the progress (x/y, time left, current set), and you can stop the job.
- Shops are queried **in parallel** per set (each shop itself stays calm and sequential).
- **Paused shops** (after a block) are skipped without clearing their last price. When starting you can choose to try anyway.
- When a job finishes you get a message and the event `lego_tracker_job_finished`.

## LEGO.com as the first source
For **RRP, image and name** LEGO.com comes first. The integration looks up the official product page (country configurable, default `nl-be`), takes the regular price as RRP (also during a promotion), the official image and the name, and tracks the page as shop **LEGO.com**, so a LEGO.com promotion counts as a deal. If the set is marked as "retiring soon" there, it gets the *retiring soon* badge. Theme, year and pieces then come from Brickset / Rebrickable. Values you entered yourself are never overwritten.

**Set data** fallback: the LEGO set database (no network) → LEGO.com → Brickset API (key) → Rebrickable API (free key via rebrickable.com → Account → API) → the public Brickset page (no key). If one source fails or misses something, the next one fills it in. The expected retirement date comes from LEGO.com, the market data or your own date.

## Notifications
*Manage → Notifications* works with rules, almost entirely with dropdowns:
1. **For which sets**: all, watchlist, my collection, certain themes, or certain sets (choose a theme first, then the set, or type any set number).
2. **When**: all-time low, discount ≥ x %, target price reached, price below € x, price drop ≥ x %, deal score ≥ x, retiring soon, back in stock, any price change; general: daily digest, new LEGO set announced, deal on a set you don't follow, job finished, problems (shop paused, errors). Optionally limited to certain shops.
3. **To whom and how**: 📱 Home Assistant app (choose a device; with image and link), ✉️ e-mail (SMTP or another notify service, free addresses), 💬 any notify service (Telegram, Signal…), 📣 notify entities, 🔔 Home Assistant notification, 🔊 speech on a speaker (TTS + media player), ⚡ only the event `lego_tracker_notification`.
4. **Extras**: don't notify again within x hours, quiet hours (notifications are bundled and sent afterwards), include image.

Every price notification (and the daily digest, per set) contains a direct link to the product page at the shop where the set is cheapest, so one tap opens the item itself; the price and shop in the message belong to that same offer. A rule limited to certain shops links to one of those shops. Sets left out under *Deals → Settings* give no deal or price notifications, except in rules for sets you picked one by one.

Every rule has a test button; sent notifications appear in the logbook.

## Settings in the panel
Everything is under *Manage → Settings* (administrators only); the integration options in Home Assistant keep working too:
- **language**, and the time of the **daily digest** (what counts as a deal is under *Deals → Settings*);
- **automatic price checks** (spread / off), the cycle and the watchlist cycle;
- **collection value**: shop price first, or the stored value first (the market value, or the value from your CSV import);
- **price-comparison sites** (on by default): per set, Kieskeurig, Shoparize, Channable and Producthero are read at most every 6 hours. A shop that fails itself (e.g. bol.com) gets the comparison price, a shop without a link gets one, and the set window lists every shop the sites know. Accessories such as LED kits are skipped. A site that doesn't have a set is not asked again within a day; after 5 network errors in a row a site is paused for an hour. Switch the whole source or single sites off, see their status, test a site with a set, or fetch all sites for all sets at once. When a site refuses the server, the userscript fetches the page in your browser;
- **ticker**: on/off and the number of items for watchlist prices, deal notifications and news (default 3 each);
- **API keys** for Brickset and Rebrickable, with a test button. Keys are never sent back to the browser (only `••••1234`);
- **bol.com API** (client id and secret, country) and the **browser relay** (on/off, at most every … hours);
- **shops**: on/off, lift a pause, *pause automatically* per shop, search URL per shop, LEGO.com country, your own shops; words that mark a product as an accessory (built-in list including LED and LMB, plus your own) and exceptions.

Pauses survive a restart, so a restart doesn't hammer a shop that just blocked us.

## Userscript (prices from your own browser)
*Manage → Userscript* has a step-by-step installation with a link to Tampermonkey for your browser. Home Assistant generates the script at `/api/lego_tracker/lego-tracker.user.js`, with your HA address and all your shops filled in, in the language you chose. The token is entered in Tampermonkey (not in the script). The script reads the price and product title of every product page you visit (on Amazon only the buy-box price) and sends them to Home Assistant; only already tracked products are updated.

### Continuous check: the userscript keeps checking by itself
Shops block servers far more often than browsers. With the continuous check switched on, the browser in which Home Assistant is open keeps checking by itself, focused on what the server can't do:

1. **Links that never had a price** (at most once an hour each).
2. **Open errors** (the list under *Logbook → Open errors* and *Shops & jobs*): the link is fetched in your browser, at most once an hour each. A price solves the error: it disappears from the lists by itself, also when you had ignored it. Suspicious prices wait for your approval.
3. **Sets without any price at all**: the shops without a link are searched in your browser. A product found there must pass the same title check (set number, LEGO, no accessory, no knock-off); it is linked and its price page is fetched right away. A search without result is not repeated for a week.
4. **Links the server can't fetch** (blocked, paused), each at most every 6 hours.

It is calm: every site at most twice a minute, a search at most once every 2 minutes per site, 4–9 seconds between pages, and a site that blocks your browser twice is left alone for an hour. Only one tab per browser does the work. Prices that came from your browser get the mark **ⓤ** next to the price (set → Shops, and on the cards); the logbook entries have the source *relay*.

**Installation, step by step**
1. **Install Tampermonkey** in the browser you use for Home Assistant (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari; the panel links to the right store). In Chrome and Edge also switch on *Allow user scripts* for Tampermonkey (Extensions → Tampermonkey → Details), or developer mode.
2. **Install the userscript**: *Manage → Userscript → Install userscript*. Tampermonkey opens an install screen: click *Install*. The script is generated for your Home Assistant address and your shops; Tampermonkey keeps it up to date.
3. **Create a token**: your Home Assistant profile → *Security* → *Long-lived access tokens* → *Create token* (e.g. "LEGO userscript"). Copy it: you only see it once.
4. **Set the token**: Tampermonkey icon → *LEGO Price Tracker settings*. The address is filled in; paste the token. The token stays in Tampermonkey on your device, never in the script.
5. **Test**: open a product page of a set you track. A green message at the bottom right says the price was sent, and *Manage → Userscript* shows "✅ Works".
6. **Switch on the continuous check**: reload *Manage → Userscript* once and press *Switch on in this browser* (or Tampermonkey menu → *Continuous check: switch on / off*). The choice is kept: it starts by itself whenever Home Assistant is open in that browser.
7. **Optional: background tabs** for shops that build their search with JavaScript (e.g. Smyths Toys): tick *Search shops that build their results with JavaScript in a background tab* under the continuous check (or Tampermonkey menu → *Background tabs for JavaScript shops*). A tab opens in the background for a few seconds, the script reads the finished page there and the tab closes. When Tampermonkey updates the script it asks you once to allow opening tabs (`GM_openInTab`).
8. **Keep it running**: the check only runs while a Home Assistant tab is open. Pin that tab, and keep the browser from putting it to sleep: Chrome → Settings → Performance → Memory Saver → *Always keep these sites active* → your Home Assistant address; Edge → Settings → System and performance → Sleeping tabs → *Never put these sites to sleep*. A computer that sleeps pauses the check; it continues when it wakes up.
9. **Check that it works**: *Manage → Userscript* shows "a browser is checking" with its last sign of life, what it is doing now and how many links without a price, open errors, searches and failing links are waiting.

**If it doesn't work**: "userscript not detected" → reload the page and check *Allow user scripts* and that the script is enabled in the Tampermonkey dashboard; "no token" or HTTP 401 → set the token again; a shop keeps failing → open it under *Shops & jobs* to see why (some shops load their search results with JavaScript, then paste the product link yourself); nothing happens → the browser relay may be off under Settings, or there is nothing to do (it looks again every 10 minutes).

## Updating your collection via CSV
Import your new export again under *Manage → Import* (Brickset, Rebrickable, a collection site or your own spreadsheet; comma, semicolon or tab). The file is checked first without saving anything. Existing sets are updated (quantity, paid, **current value**, condition, location), new sets are added. With *update afterwards* (default on) a job fills in the data per set, finds missing shop links and fetches the prices. Every changed value is kept as history, so the growth chart follows your repeated imports.

## Link check
*Manage → Link check* judges every shop link:
- **✓ correct**: the set number is in the product title (or URL), together with "LEGO".
- **⚠ suspicious**: an accessory (lighting, display case, stickers…), a knock-off brand (Keeppley, Mould King, Cada…), the set number is missing, or the price is far too low for the set.
- **? not checked**: no title known yet (for Amazon it comes with the next price check).

Suspicious links **don't count** for prices, lowest price ever and collection value until you approve them. A removed link is never linked automatically again.

## Deal score (0–100)
Discount vs. RRP (max 45) + closeness to the lowest price ever (max 25) + discount vs. the 90-day median (max 20) + target price reached (10). ≥ 70 = top deal, ≥ 45 = good deal.

## Validation
- **CSV import**: invalid set number, quantity 0 or negative, negative prices (error, line skipped); unlikely year / pieces / price, price > 3× RRP, unknown or future date, date before the release year, too long text (warning, field ignored); duplicate lines (merged as extra copies); already in the collection (updated). Max 2 MB / 5000 lines. Column names in several languages.
- **Editing** in the panel: numbers and dates are validated (no negative amounts, no future purchase date, quantity ≥ 1); amounts accept a decimal comma.
- **Lost decimal comma**: amounts stored by older versions without their comma (e.g. 16499 for 164,99) are repaired on start, but only when the shop prices (or, for a purchase price, the RRP) show it; a real high amount stays as it is.
- **Restoring a backup**: structure, set numbers, URLs and price history are checked before anything changes; data from older versions is brought up to date.
- **CSV export** is protected against formula injection.

## Sensors, events and services
- **Sensors**: tracked sets, sets at all-time low, sets with high discount, sets at target price, best deal score (top 5 in the attributes), sets retiring soon, wishlist cost, offers with errors, collection value / cost / growth, plus a price sensor per set.
- **Events**: `lego_tracker_new_all_time_low`, `lego_tracker_high_discount`, `lego_tracker_target_price_reached`, `lego_tracker_daily_digest`, `lego_tracker_job_finished`, `lego_tracker_notification`. Deal events carry the direct product link at the cheapest shop; sets left out under *Deals → Settings* give no deal events.
- **Services**: `add_set`, `add_sets`, `remove_set`, `set_offer`, `fix_offer`, `report_price`, `discover_offers`, `refresh`, `enrich_sets`, `import_collection`, `verify_links`, `confirm_offer`, `remove_offer`, `cancel_job`, `export_collection`, `export_data`, `import_data`, `send_digest`.

## bol.com: three ways that work
bol.com blocks almost every server (HTTP 403), so scraping it from Home Assistant rarely works. There are better routes:

1. **bol.com API.** bol.com offers a free Marketing Catalog API to members of its affiliate program (Partnerprogramma). Sign up at [partner.bol.com](https://partner.bol.com), create API credentials (client id + secret) and paste them under *Manage → Settings → bol.com API*; the **Test** button looks up set 10281 to check them. From then on bol.com links are found through the API's search (the product title must pass the link check) and prices come from the API's *best offer*, per country (NL or BE, automatic from your LEGO.com country). No scraping, no blocks.
2. **Browser relay (no account needed).** With the userscript installed, the browser in which you have Home Assistant open fetches the shop pages that fail on the server — bol.com, Amazon or any other shop — in the background, calmly one by one (4–9 s apart), and sends the prices to Home Assistant. Your browser is a normal visitor with your own connection and cookies, so it is rarely blocked. It runs automatically at most every 6 hours (configurable, or off), or right away with *Manage → Userscript → Run now in this browser*, which also shows the progress. If a shop blocks your browser too, the relay stops asking that shop for the rest of the run and logs it.
3. **Price-comparison sites.** When bol.com itself fails, its price comes from a comparison site that lists it (marked ⓢ / ⓚ / ⓒ / ⓟ).

All are logged in the logbook (source *bol.com API*, *relay* or the comparison site), and the per-shop **↻ Fetch** buttons use the API too. Not verified against the live bol.com API yet: the response parsing is deliberately tolerant, and the Test button tells you right away whether it works.

## Blocks (captcha / HTTP 403) and what helps
Amazon and bol.com mainly recognise scrapers by their **TLS fingerprint**, not their headers. Three layers:
1. **Imitate Chrome** (default on): the integration installs `curl_cffi` and makes requests with Chrome's fingerprint, a home-page visit, cookies per shop and real browser headers. If the installation fails it falls back to aiohttp.
2. **Pause after a block**: a blocked shop is left alone for 1 h, 3 h, 6 h, 12 h, then 24 h. Hammering makes the protection stricter. The spread schedule also keeps the request rate low.
3. **Deliver prices from outside** (always works): the bol.com API, the browser relay and the userscript, the price-comparison sites (see *bol.com: three ways that work*), the service `lego_tracker.report_price` (for n8n or automations), or a manual price in the set dialog.

## Limitations
- To protect the traffic to and the load on the shops and comparison sites, every site is asked at most twice a minute and searched at most once every 2 minutes. Manual actions follow the same rules: *Fetch prices* of a set once every 2 minutes, *Find* / *Fetch* of a shop once every 2 minutes per shop; the buttons are greyed out (with a countdown) until then. *Find links* at the top is a job you can follow and stop, spread out by the same rules.
- Shop markup changes; parsers try JSON-LD → meta tags → shop-specific markup. See `tests/test_logic.py`.
